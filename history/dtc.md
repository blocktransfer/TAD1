# Depository Trust Company

On September 15, 1972, drafts of DTC's articles of incorporation, bylaws, and rules were filed with the Commission for review, as well as with state and federal banking authorities. [BASIC, *Interindustry Teamwork* (Apr. 1, 1974), p. 42](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1980/1984_0401_BasicTeamwork_1.pdf).

From September 1972 through early May 1973, CCS and Commission staff discussed and revised the proposed documents. They met on September 29; revised papers were refiled on October 18; the Commission publicly solicited comments on November 8; and ten comment letters were received. Further conferences and written submissions preceded the Commission's May 8, 1973 conditional clearance, subject to specified changes. [BASIC, *Interindustry Teamwork*, pp. 41–42](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1980/1984_0401_BasicTeamwork_1.pdf).

Contemporary congressional testimony states that, after the New York Superintendent of Banks and Banking Board approved DTC's organization certificate, the Depository Trust Company (DTC) took over the assets and operations of Central Certificate Service, Inc. on May 11, 1973. The same testimony states that DTC's application for Federal Reserve System membership was approved on April 19, 1973 and became effective on June 27, 1973.

William Dentzer testified that CCS became “The Depository Trust Company” on May 11, 1973; he also reported Federal Reserve membership approval and that the SEC had virtually approved DTC's rules package. DTC was initially a wholly owned subsidiary of the New York Stock Exchange.

The [July 11–12, 1973 Senate hearings on clearing agencies and transfer agents, p. 209](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-93shrg98978O/pdf/CHRG-93shrg98978O.pdf#page=209) document the organization-certificate approval, operational takeover and Federal Reserve membership milestones. The [other Senate testimony](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-93shrg98978O/CHRG-93shrg98978O) uses the “known as DTC since May 11” wording for that transition.


DTC's [1973 annual report, p. 3 (PDF p. 5)](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1970/1973_0101_DTCAR.pdf#page=5) discusses the operational transition and original NYSE ownership.

The [New York State Banking Department's 2001 annual report, Schedule B, Part 2, p. 4 of 14 (PDF pp. 118 and 133)](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2020/02/bd_areport_2001.pdf#page=118) independently lists May 11, 1973 as DTC's "Date of Charter or License." Because the column combines two different regulatory events, that listing does not, by itself, establish a distinct legal incorporation date.

DTC's [1973 annual report](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1970/1973_0101_DTCAR.pdf#page=18) documents the transfer and original ownership. The [New York State Banking Department's 2001 annual report](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2020/02/bd_areport_2001.pdf) independently records May 11, 1973 for DTC. For contemporary legislative context, see the [July 1973 Senate hearings](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-93shrg98978O/CHRG-93shrg98978O), which occurred after the transfer.

CCS ownership transfer to Series A preferred shares came on 4 Nov 1999.
